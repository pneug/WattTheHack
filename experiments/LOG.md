# Experiment log

Val (4-9 Sep, README-mandated) is the secondary number. Entries before the note below were decided on the per-turbine energy deviation.

- Run: `python -m pipeline.run <id>` (or `--all`); history in `experiments/results.csv`, details in `experiments/runs/`.
- Add an experiment: `derive(parent_id, desc, **changed_stages)` in `pipeline/experiments.py`, register it in `CONFIGS`.
- The leaderboard between the markers is generated by `pipeline/report.py`; do not edit it by hand.

### E00 baseline (OLS on park means, static)
Change: T = a + b*H + c*O per turbine, fitted on all data outside the window; point power; Gaussian band.
Result: bt ws RMSE 1.261, P RMSE 737 kW; mean |E dev| 13.8 % (park 11.0 %, p90 28.5 %); val ws RMSE 1.405, park E dev +0.07 %.
Insight: bt bias is negative on all turbines (-8.6/-5.4/-7.2 %): P(v_hat) underestimates energy (Jensen, cubic curve). Val park +0.07 % hides T1 +18.6 % and T2/T3 -5.4/-8.2 %; the 95 % band covers 94.6 % (bt).
Decision: reference point.

### E01 time weighting (exp decay 3-30 d, local windows 7-30 d)
Change: OLS fit weighted by distance to the outage (both sides). Rest = E00.
Result: mean |E dev| 13.8 -> 13.8-14.7 %; ws RMSE unchanged (1.260-1.267).
Insight: the month-to-month drift of a, b (notebook 11c+) does not help locally; less data only adds noise.
Decision: drop.

### E02 smoothed references (30/60/120 min)
Change: centred rolling mean of H and O as input. Rest = E00.
Result: ws RMSE 1.261 -> 1.217 (120 min), P RMSE 737 -> 710 kW; mean |E dev| unchanged or worse (13.8-14.2 %).
Insight: averaging out reference gusts helps 10-min accuracy but not the energy sum.
Decision: superseded by lags (E06), which do the same and let the fit choose the weights.

### E03 power conversion: E[P(v)] instead of P(v_hat)
Change: average the power curve over the predictive distribution (Gaussian sigma or empirical residuals). Rest = E00.
Result: bias -8.6/-5.4/-7.2 % -> +7.0/+4.4/+4.8 % (Gaussian), +5.2/+3.1/+3.1 % (empirical); park |E dev| 11.0 -> 9.3-9.6 %, P RMSE 737 -> 724 kW.
Insight: P(v_hat) is biased low because the power curve is convex at 3-11 m/s. A single sigma over-corrects, because the spread at low wind is smaller than the average.
Decision: keep, with sigma per wind-speed bin (E09) and on the better wind model (E11).

### E04 variance-ratio MCP
Change: rescale OLS so the prediction has T's mean and std. Rest = E00.
Result: bias close to 0 (-2 %), but ws RMSE 1.261 -> 1.328 and val park E dev +13.4 %.
Insight: inflating the spread fixes the average bias but adds noise in every step; E[P(v)] fixes the bias without hurting the wind prediction.
Decision: drop.

### E05 nonlinearity and heteroscedasticity
Change: hinges (3/6/9 m/s), log1p, sqrt transforms; separately sigma per wind-speed bin. Rest = E00.
Result: transforms do not help (mean |E dev| 13.9-14.8 %, log val park -13.5 %). Sigma in 5 bins: coverage 94.6 -> 95.1 % (bt), 90.7 -> 95.0 % (val), band narrower (4.88 -> 4.82 m/s).
Insight: the relation is linear enough; the spread is not constant (it grows with wind speed).
Decision: keep sigma bins; drop transforms.

### E06 secondary corrections (one at a time)
Change: + hour (x H), direction (sin/cos, 4 or 8 sectors x H), lags -20..+20 min, H stddev, temperature, pressure.
Result: direction is the only clear energy gain (mean |E dev| 13.8 -> 11.8 % with 8 sectors); lags give the best ws RMSE (1.224); hour, stddev, temperature, pressure: no gain (13.8-14.3 %).
Insight: wind direction changes how T relates to H (terrain/wake); timing helps a little; the environmental extras carry no information beyond wind speed.
Decision: keep direction (8 sectors) and lags.

### E07 calibrated uncertainty
Change: 95 % band from out-of-sample (backtest) residuals: Gaussian, conformal, conformal per bin.
Result: coverage 94.6 % (E00) -> 94.7-94.8 %; per bin 94.7 % with a narrower band (4.74 m/s).
Insight: the training sigma was already well calibrated on average; the 89-91 % in the val window is that window alone. Binning matters more than out-of-sample calibration.
Decision: use training residuals with sigma bins (simpler, same coverage). For the kWh interval, use the backtest energy errors (see E11).

### E08 challengers
Change: ridge on all 7 references; gradient boosting (all refs + hour + direction + stddev).
Result: ridge 12.8 %; HGB 10.8 % mean |E dev|, P RMSE 700 kW, but coverage 89 % and bias -4..-7 %.
Insight: the black box gains less than the transparent features. All 7 references help a little over the park means.
Decision: use as a reference only.

### E10/E11 stacked: direction (8) + lags + sigma bins + E[P(v)]  (BEST)
Change: features park means + direction octant (x H) + lags -20..+20 min; sigma per wind-speed bin (5); E[P(v)].
Result: vs E00: ws RMSE 1.261 -> 1.193 (-5 %), P RMSE 737 -> 680 kW (-8 %), mean |E dev| 13.8 -> 10.0 %, bias +2.2/+1.2/+2.1 %, coverage 95 %. Beats HGB on every metric. Val: ws RMSE 1.405 -> 1.329, park E dev +2.3 %.
Insight: the energy gain needs both parts: a better wind model (direction, lags) and the correct power conversion. Each alone stays near 11.8 % / 14.0 %.
kWh interval (95 %, from the 28 backtest park errors): -15 % / +30 %, skewed because calm windows give large relative errors. Hidden 1-3 Sep: 320.7 MWh (247.6-375.9).
Decision: BEST. Next ideas: all 7 refs + direction + lags (ridge), pooled direction effects across turbines.

### E12 explainable variant: 60-min smoothed references instead of lags  (BEST)
Change: E11 with the lags replaced by a centred 60-min mean of H and O.
Result: mean |E dev| 9.99 % (E11 9.98 %), P RMSE 682 kW (680), ws RMSE 1.200 (1.193), coverage 95 %; val park E dev +2.5 %.
Insight: same accuracy. With lags the H weight is split over 5 collinear columns (H itself 0.05), which cannot be explained; smoothing keeps one number per park and sector.
Decision: BEST (explainability wins ties). Hidden 1-3 Sep: see `python -m pipeline.submit`.

### Note: decision metric switched to the park total
Change: the judges score energy as the absolute deviation of the park total (the whole park is out; insurance pays the total). Leaderboard now sorts by bt park |E dev|.
Result: park |E dev| E00 11.0 % -> E12 8.5 %; the top 4 configs are 8.2-8.6 %, within the noise (std per outage ~11 %, so +-2 pp on a 28-outage mean).
Insight: at park level the gain is almost all bias removal (-8.5 % -> +1.4 %, noise unchanged). The power fix (E[P(v)]) is the main lever; direction sectors help per turbine and on RMSE but hardly on the park total (11.0 -> 10.9 %), because turbine errors cancel in the sum.
Decision: keep E12 as BEST (most explainable of the top 4, best power RMSE after E11).
